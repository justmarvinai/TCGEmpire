# Yu-Gi-Oh! — Legend of Blue Eyes White Dragon (LOB)

> - **Origin (JP):** the TCG set merges two Japanese OCG boosters: "Legend of Blue Eyes White Dragon" (18 May 2000, 61 cards, 5 per pack) and "Phantom God" (2000, exact date (?)).
> - **Release:** North America 8 March 2002 (the first TCG booster ever). Europe: English December 2002 (?), French March 2003, **German 25 April 2003 (LOB-G)**, Italian 12 September 2003, Spanish October 2003.
> - **Set size:** 126 cards (LOB-000 to LOB-125) = 82 Common (incl. Short Prints), 22 Rare, 10 Super Rare, 10 Ultra Rare, 2 Secret Rare. **The 2003 German print has only 103 cards** (see "German print" below).
> - **Booster:** 9 cards per pack. Pack artwork / cover card: Blue-Eyes White Dragon.
> - **Display:** 24 boosters per box (confirmed for the North American 1st Edition box and the 2023 reprint; German 2003 display size (?)).
> - **MSRP then:** not verifiable. Often remembered as about US$3.29 per booster (?) and roughly 4 € in Germany (?). For reference, the 2023 reprint has an official MSRP of US$4.49 per pack.
> - **Print variants:** *1st Edition* (German: "1. Auflage") = the first, limited print run, marked with an edition stamp, far scarcer. *Unlimited* = later open-ended reprints without the stamp. *"Wavy" 1st Edition* = collector term for the earliest North American 1st Edition run, named after its wavy foil look (?). *LOB-G* = the German-language European print with its own numbering (LOB-G000 to LOB-G102).
> - **Reprints:** Legendary Collection (LC01, 2010), Gameboard Edition (September 2018), 25th Anniversary Edition (20 April 2023, all 126 cards in German as LOB-DE000 to LOB-DE125).

Tier logic: the real Ultra Rares that are not top chases, both real Secret Rares and the biggest Super Rare spells are Holo. The remaining real Super Rares and the most popular real Rares are Rare. Real "Short Print" commons and the other real Rares are Uncommon. Mystische Elfe is a real Super Rare, so it sits in Rare, not in the Common/Uncommon filler.

## Card list

How to read `Real #/rarity`:

- `LOB-###` is the number in the 126-card North American / worldwide list. The 2023 German reprint uses the same numbers as `LOB-DE###`.
- `G###` is the number in the 103-card German 2003 print (LOB-G###), confirmed in the German set list.
- `G###°` is derived from the renumbering rule (same order, gaps closed) and not individually confirmed.
- `G—` means the card was **not** in the German 2003 LOB print; it shipped later in the European Magic Ruler set.

German names follow the German set lists on Yugipedia / Yu-Gi-Oh! Wiki (original LOB-G list and the 2023 LOB-DE list). ATK/DEF, attributes and card kinds are standard card data that could not be re-checked online in this session (see Open points). Desirability is our own estimate of collector and nostalgia pull inside this set.

### German print (LOB-G): what was missing

The European English, French, German and Italian prints dropped 23 of the 126 cards and renumbered the rest without gaps. The 23 cards came later in the European Magic Ruler set (numbers MRL-E103 to E129 on the European English print), all as Commons except Topf der Gier, which stayed Rare.

- Confirmed missing from LOB-G (21): Hitotsu-Me Giant, Flame Swordsman, Mammoth Graveyard, Trial of Nightmare, Fusionist, Petit Angel, Mystical Sheep #2, Masaki the Legendary Swordsman, Kurama, Power of Kaishin, Mountain, Hinotama, Beaver Warrior, Karbonala Warrior, Larvas, M-Warrior #1, M-Warrior #2, Frenzied Panda, One-Eyed Shield Dragon, Hane-Hane, Pot of Greed.
- Deduced from the numbering (1): Dragoness the Wicked Knight.
- Unknown (1): one card between LOB-106 and LOB-112 (Armed Ninja, Flower Wolf, Man-Eater Bug, Sand Stone, Misairuzame or Steel Ogre Grotto #1).
- 13 cards of our list are affected (marked `G—`). Our set uses the full 126-card pool, as the 2023 German reprint does.

## Community lore & meme material

Items marked (?) contain a detail that could not be confirmed in this session.

- **Exodia wins on the spot.** Holding all five "Verbotene" pieces wins the duel instantly; Yugi beats Kaiba this way in anime episode 1 ("Exodia, obliterate!" in the English dub).
- **Exodia goes overboard.** On the ship to Duelist Kingdom, Weevil Underwood throws Yugi's Exodia cards into the sea, which is why Yugi never uses the combo again.
- **Kaiba rips the fourth Blue-Eyes.** In the story only four Blue-Eyes exist; Kaiba owns three and tears up Grandpa Muto's copy so it can never be played against him.
- **Anime rules are made up.** Early duels ignore the real rulebook: Blue-Eyes is summoned without Tributes, terrain gives stat bonuses, and Yugi beats Mako by attacking the moon to drain the sea.
- **Flame Swordsman's two lives.** Joey plays it as a regular monster in the anime; the real card is a Fusion of Flame Manipulator and Masaki.
- **"Topf der Gier — was macht die Karte?"** Running fan joke: characters keep explaining that Pot of Greed "lets me draw two cards".
- **Too good to be legal.** Pot of Greed has been Forbidden in the TCG for many years (since 2005 (?)).
- **Ban-list classics.** Raigeki, Schwarzes Loch (Dark Hole) and Wiedergeburt (Monster Reborn) all spent long stretches on the Forbidden or Limited list (exact dates (?)).
- **"Herz der Karten."** Yugi's "Heart of the Cards" became community shorthand for top-decking exactly the card you need.
- **"Screw the rules, I have money!"** A Kaiba line from the fan parody *Yu-Gi-Oh! The Abridged Series* (2006), not from the real show, and still one of the most quoted lines in the hobby.
- **The 1st Edition Blue-Eyes.** LOB-001 in 1st Edition is the set's top card; PSA 10 copies have reportedly sold for five-figure dollar sums (record price and population figures (?)).
- **"Wavy" boxes.** Collectors single out the earliest North American 1st Edition run as "wavy"; sealed wavy displays of 24 packs are sold through auction houses.
- **Germany got it late and short.** LOB-G arrived on 25 April 2003, over a year after the US launch, with 103 of 126 cards; the cut reportedly sparked fan protests in Europe.
- **A Common Flame Swordsman.** The 23 held-back cards, including Flammen-Schwertkämpfer and Hitotsu-Me-Riese, later appeared in European Magic Ruler as Commons; only Topf der Gier kept its Rare status.
- **Translation quirks.** "Final Flame" became "Ewige Flamme" (eternal, not final), "Swords of Revealing Light" became "Verräterische Schwerter", and "Goblin's Secret Remedy" became "Kobolds letzte Rettung".
- **Half-translated names.** "Feueryarou" and "Turtle-Tiger" translate only part of the English name, and "Gaia The Fierce Knight" is "Gaia, zorniger Ritter" (the angry knight).
- **"Blauäugiger w. Drache."** The official German name abbreviates "weißer" to "w." so it fits the name bar; it is listed that way for LOB-G001 and for the 2010 reprint (LC01-DE004).
- **Wiedergeburt vs. Monster-Reanimation.** The German card is "Wiedergeburt", but the German anime dub of the original series and GX called it "Monster-Reanimation".
- **The ankh.** Monster Reborn's original Japanese artwork shows an ankh; international prints got redrawn art, the basis for our Alternative version.
- **Short Prints.** Some LOB commons, mostly Equip Spells such as Schwert der Legende, Buch der geheimen Künste and Dunkle Energie, were printed in lower numbers and are harder to pull than their rarity suggests.

# Pokémon — Fossil

Curated in-game version of the real Pokémon TCG expansion Fossil, the second English expansion: **75 cards** (all 62 real cards + 13 alternate-art cards), mapped onto the game's 7 tiers. "(?)" marks anything that could not be verified.

- **Release:** Japan 21 Jun 1997 as "Mystery of the Fossils" (化石の秘密, 48 cards including Mew; 10 cards per pack) · USA 10 Oct 1999 (Wizards of the Coast) · Germany October 2000 as "Fossil" (PokéWiki; exact day not found (?)), four months after Dschungel.
- **Real set size:** 62 cards = 15 Holo Rare (1–15), the same 15 Pokémon again as non-holo Rare with identical artwork (16–30), 16 Uncommon (31–45 and the Trainer Mr. Fuji, 58), 16 Common (46–57 and the Trainers 59–62). No Energy cards. Pokémon blocks are numbered alphabetically by English name.
- **Booster and display:** 11 cards per booster with 1 Rare, holo or non-holo (Bill's Archive); the split 7 Common / 3 Uncommon / 1 Rare is assumed to match Jungle (?). Holo in about 1 of 3 packs (community estimate; one seller claims 1 in 5 (?)). 36 boosters per display.
- **Booster artwork:** three English wrappers, one each with Lapras, Aerodactyl and Zapdos; Aerodactyl alone is on the display box. The Japanese pack (art by Mitsuhiro Arita) shows a group including Kabutops, Zapdos and Golbat (?).
- **Original price:** the booster MSRP was not found; most likely about US$3.29 like the Base Set (?). German DM price not found (?).
- **Print runs:** 1st Edition (stamp) and Unlimited, in English and in German ("1. Edition").
- **Theme decks:** BodyGuard / "Leibwächter" (Pflanze/Kampf, holo Sleimok) and LockDown / "Schloss und Riegel" (Feuer/Wasser, holo Lapras): 60 cards, coin, 10 damage counters, rulebook.
- **Milestone:** with the Base Set and Jungle, Fossil completed the original 150 Pokémon in the English TCG, and it holds the first Ditto card. The Japanese set also had Mew; the English one moved it to a promo.

Notes on the mapping:

- Nothing is cut. The real set has only 47 different cards, so every real card is in and every evolution line inside the set stays complete (including the fossil lines: Geheimnis-Fossil → Kabuto → Kabutops, Amonitas → Amoroso, Aerodactyl).
- All 15 real Holo Rares are in: 8 as Holo, 5 as Ultra, 2 as Grail.
- The Rare tier is the 15 real non-holo Rares, the "twins" of the holos with the same artwork, named "(Non-Holo)". Art: a twin reuses its holo's kit spec without foil; the twins of Ultra and Grail cards get a simple kit version of the hand-made motif. No new drawing.
- Uncommon and Common follow the real rarity exactly: Mr. Fuji is the only Uncommon Trainer, the other four Trainers are Common. Fossil has no Energy cards, so the booster needs no energy slot.
- **Two Grails:** Dragoran and Gengar lead every Fossil price list (1st Edition PSA 10 about €7,350–8,100; the next card, Lapras, about half of that) and swap first place between sources, so both get d 10.
- **Five Ultras:** the three wrapper stars Lapras, Aerodactyl and Zapdos, plus Arktos and Lavados, which complete the legendary bird trio and rank third to fifth in the German price list. Ditto would be the sixth; it stays the top Holo so that the Holo tier keeps 8 cards.
- **Alternatives:** Aerodactyl (Prerelease) is named after the real prerelease promo but gets game-original art; the other twelve are game-original alternate arts of fan favourites, including both Grails and four Ultras.
- **Cross-set names:** Raichu, Magneton, Zapdos, Alpollo and Nebulak also exist in our Basis-Set (Zapdos is a Holo there and an Ultra here); Magmar was cut from our Basis-Set and appears here.

## Grail and Ultra art briefs (hand-made, full art)

Drawn by hand (up to four review rounds, `ART_DIRECTION.md`). These are our own compositions, not copies of the real card art. Each card's Non-Holo twin is a kit version of the same motif; its Alt-Art must read as a clearly different picture.

**Grail**

- *Dragoran* (Grail, Farblos, 075). **Pose:** flying towards the viewer out of a sea storm, small wings in a strong upstroke, arms open wide, calm friendly face, antennae swept back by the wind. **Scene:** night sea with towering waves; a lighthouse beam cuts through the rain and lights the dragon from the side; a tiny boat silhouette rides the swell behind it (a nod to its Pokédex lore as the rescuer of shipwrecked crews). **Colours:** warm orange body, cream belly bands, teal-green wing membranes, navy and slate-grey sea, gold lighthouse light; rainbow-gold full-art frame. **Why Grail:** first in the German and Dutch price lists; the friendliest dragon in Kanto.
- *Gengar* (Grail, Psycho, 074). **Pose:** leaning half out of a cracked full-length mirror, one clawed hand gripping the frame, enormous toothy grin, red eyes glowing, spiky silhouette. **Scene:** abandoned mansion hallway at night, moonlight through a broken window, a candelabra with guttering green-tinted flames, shadows that do not match their objects. **Colours:** deep violet body, crimson eyes, white teeth, black shadows, sickly green candle glow, silver moonlight; rainbow-gold full-art frame. **Why Grail:** one of the most loved Kanto Pokémon, the most valuable raw Fossil card on Card Codex, and its PSA 10 sales are level with Dragoran's.

**Ultra**

- *Lapras* (Ultra, Wasser, 070). **Pose:** swimming from left to right, long neck raised, mouth open mid-song, small rings of sound in the air. **Scene:** misty sea at dawn, islands on the horizon, a few seabirds, its reflection broken by soft waves. **Colours:** sky blue body, cream belly, grey knobbed shell, peach-pink dawn, pale gold. **Why:** wrapper star, the LockDown theme-deck holo and third in the price lists.
- *Arktos* (Ultra, Wasser, 071). **Pose:** soaring diagonally across the card, wings fully spread, the long ribbon tail curving behind, head turned to the viewer. **Scene:** frozen mountain peaks under a night sky with aurora; ice crystals form in its wake (its attacks Freeze Dry and Blizzard). **Colours:** icy blue, white, navy night, aurora green, silver. **Why:** fourth in the price lists; one of the three legendary birds.
- *Lavados* (Ultra, Feuer, 069). **Pose:** rising straight up out of a lava crater, wings of fire spread to full width, head thrown back mid-cry. **Scene:** volcanic caldera, lava splashes, black rock, ash plumes against the sky. **Colours:** golden yellow body, orange-red flames, black and charcoal rock, crimson sky. **Why:** completes the bird trio; high in the German price list.
- *Zapdos* (Ultra, Elektro, 072). **Pose:** perched on the top of a storm-wrecked radio tower, jagged wings raised, beak open in a screech, bolts converging on it from the clouds. **Scene:** night thunderstorm over dark hills, rain streaks, sparks raining down from the tower. **Colours:** yellow and black, white-blue lightning, purple clouds. Must read differently from the Basis-Set Zapdos (a dive through black clouds). **Why:** wrapper star; completes the bird trio.
- *Aerodactyl* (Ultra, Kampf, 073). **Pose:** bursting out of a cliff face full of fossils, wings spread, jaws wide showing its fangs, rubble and amber chunks flying. **Scene:** prehistoric canyon at sunset, ferns, spiral fossils in the rock layers. **Colours:** lavender-grey skin, deep red mouth, amber, orange sky, warm brown rock. **Why:** wrapper star, the only Pokémon on the display box and the face of the set.

## Community lore & meme material

- **The 150 complete:** with the Base Set and Jungle, Fossil put all of the original 150 Pokémon into the English TCG. The Japanese "Mystery of the Fossils" also contained Mew (151); in English, Mew became a Pokémon League promo instead (Bill's Archive).
- **First Ditto:** Fossil holds the very first Ditto card.
- **Prerelease Aerodactyl:** Fossil's prerelease card was Aerodactyl with a "PRERELEASE" stamp; Aerodactyl is also on a wrapper and alone on the display box.
- **Gambler:** "Spieler" shuffles your hand into your deck, then a coin decides: heads, draw 8 cards; tails, draw 1. The hobby on one Trainer card.
- **A rock with 10 HP:** Mysterious Fossil ("Geheimnis-Fossil") is played like a Basic Pokémon with 10 HP and gives no prize card when it is knocked out; Kabuto, Omanyte and Aerodactyl evolve from it (PokéWiki).
- **Lock cards:** Aerodactyl's "Prehistoric Power" stops all evolution while it is in play; Muk's "Toxic Gas" switches off every Pokémon Power, including the Base Set's Rain Dance and Damage Swap; Psyduck's "Headache" bans the opponent's Trainer cards for a turn.
- **The self-destruct club:** Golem, Weezing and Magneton all carry a Selfdestruct attack that also hits their own bench.
- **Gengar's Curse:** its power moves damage counters between the opponent's Pokémon. Today Gengar is the most valuable raw Fossil card on Card Codex (about US$725), and 1st Edition PSA 10 copies sold for US$6,500 and US$6,000 in March and April 2025 (Sports Card Investor).
- **Dragonite on top:** German and Dutch price lists put a 1st Edition PSA 10 Dragonite at about €7,750–8,100, Gengar at about €7,350, Lapras up to €3,800, Articuno up to €3,500 and Moltres up to €1,500 (Pokezentrum, rarecards.nl; snapshots).
- **Theme decks:** BodyGuard ("Leibwächter", holo Muk) and LockDown ("Schloss und Riegel", holo Lapras), each with 60 cards, a coin, 10 damage counters and a rulebook; per Bulbapedia the last decks that shipped glass damage counters.
- **Misprint hunters:** Elite Fourum keeps a "Master Misprint Guide" with more than 200 photos just for corrected Fossil errors, down to a small scratch on Krabby's set symbol.
- **Memes:** Zubat in every cave; Psyduck's eternal headache; Slowpoke, the slowest thinker in Kanto; Ditto's dot face; "Lord Helix", the Helix Fossil worshipped by Twitch Plays Pokémon in 2014 (it revives Omanyte); in the original games you choose either the Helix Fossil (Amonitas) or the Dome Fossil (Kabuto) and never get both.
- **German print:** Fossil came out in German in October 2000 (PokéWiki), with 1. Edition and Unlimited runs, four months after Dschungel.

A human spot check against the PokéWiki page "Fossil (TCG)" is recommended.
- **German release:** PokéWiki says October 2000; a Bulbapedia extract mentions other language releases in April 2000. Not resolved, and no exact day or DM price found (?).
- **Booster split and odds:** only "11 cards including 1 Rare" is confirmed for Fossil; 7 Common / 3 Uncommon / 1 Rare is assumed from Jungle, and the holo rate (1 in 3 vs. a seller's 1 in 5) is unconfirmed (?). German display size not verified (36 assumed (?)).
- **MSRP:** US$3.29 per booster is the Base Set figure and was not confirmed for Fossil (?).
- **Card numbers:** the holo numbers 1–15 and the Trainers 58–62 are confirmed. Non-holo = holo + 15 is confirmed for Articuno 17, Dragonite 19, Moltres 27 and Zapdos 30; Uncommons run Arbok 31 to Weezing 45 and Commons Ekans 46 to Zubat 57. Numbers inside those blocks follow the alphabetical rule (?).
- **German names:** the five Trainer names (Mr. Fuji, Energiesuche, Spieler, Wiederverwertung, Geheimnis-Fossil) come from PokéWiki extracts; the Pokémon names are the standard German species names, not checked against German card scans (?).
- **Unconfirmed lore details:** the Japanese pack art (Mitsuhiro Arita; Kabutops, Zapdos, Golbat), the exact Mew promo dates, sealed 1st Edition display prices (a forum mention of US$17,100, undated, and a price list's US$78,000 in 2024) and Dragonite PSA 10 sales from 2025–2026 (US$5,160 in February 2025 and US$17,690 in August 2026 per one secondary source) (?).
- **Theme deck counters:** Bulbapedia calls the Fossil decks the last with glass damage counters, while PokéWiki calls the Jungle decks the first with punched cardboard ones; the sources conflict (?).
- **Design choices to confirm:** (1) the Rare tier as 15 "(Non-Holo)" twins; (2) two Grails, Gengar and Dragoran, with equal d 10 (equal draw weights recommended); (3) five Ultras (Lapras, Aerodactyl, the three birds) with Ditto as the top Holo instead of a sixth Ultra; (4) the 13 Alternatives, including Amonitas for the "Lord Helix" meme; (5) Raichu, Magneton, Zapdos, Alpollo and Nebulak exist in both Pokémon sets under the same German name (different ids).
